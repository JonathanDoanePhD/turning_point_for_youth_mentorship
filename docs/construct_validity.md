# Construct validity audit: the "support" measures are not interchangeable

Status: 2026-11-13, written for the independent review response. The project's four analyses and the literature it cites use
different constructs. Earlier drafts sometimes described them collectively as "adult support" and drew common conclusions. This
table keeps them apart.

## Three different "ages"

- **Age at measurement:** how old the young person was when the report was made. This is what all four completed analyses vary.
- **Age at exposure onset:** when the relationship or behavior began. **None of the completed analyses observes onset.** Only the
  Add Health mentor item (`H3MN6`, retrospective) does, and it has not been analyzed (study A3, human-run).
- **Relationship duration:** how long the relationship lasted. Not observed in any completed analysis. NLSY97 1997–2000 observes only
  *repeated reports of the same rating*, which reflect duration, stability, family characteristics and measurement error all at once.

## Consequences for the conclusions

1. The completed analyses concern **parents (supportiveness, praise, closeness) and math teachers**, not mentors. They cannot
   say when *mentoring* should start.
2. All four vary **age or grade at measurement**, not **age at onset**. A stronger association for reports at 10–14 than at 6–9
   does not show that relationships beginning at 10–14 matter more.
3. Paper 1's 1997–2000 analysis counts **repeated ratings**, not relationship-years. Equal weights across rounds are what one
   would expect from a stable family characteristic measured with error, as well as from a cumulative effect.
4. The B2 comparison is between **ratings of two different teachers**, so it is also a comparison of two relationships, not of
   one relationship at two ages.
