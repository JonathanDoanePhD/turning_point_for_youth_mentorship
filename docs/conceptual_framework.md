# Conceptual framework

Status: draft v0.1. This framework organises constructs; it does **not** assign weights to
domains and does not define a composite "turning point score" (see decision D-004).

## Core idea

Educational trajectories are shaped by repeated interactions between a young person and their
contexts (family, school, peers, community, structural conditions) over time. Supportive adult
relationships are one input among many. They may act directly (information, encouragement,
advocacy, help navigating institutions) and indirectly by strengthening identity and agency,
school engagement and psychological resources. The framework draws on life-course theory
(turning points, timing, linked lives), positive youth development and resilience research,
stage-environment fit around school transitions, and the youth-mentoring model of relationship
quality and duration; citations are in `literature_review.md`.

## Pathways (to be tested, not assumed)

```
Structural conditions ─┬──────────────► School & community context ─┐
                       │                                             │
Family environment ────┼──► Adult support (who, how much, when, how long) ──► Identity & agency ──┐
                       │                     │                                                     │
Prior achievement, ────┘                     └──► School engagement & belonging ──────────────────┼──► Educational attainment
engagement, health                                                                                 │    (and secondary outcomes)
                                    Transitions & opportunities (grade 9, course choice, PS planning) ┘
```

Timing enters in three different ways, which we model differently:

1. **Age at measurement** – the age at which support is observed (between-person variation in
   cohort studies with a range of baseline ages).
2. **Age at onset** – the age when a relationship began (available retrospectively in some
   studies; subject to recall and survivorship bias).
3. **Duration / accumulation** – how many periods support was present (life-course
   accumulation model).

## Domains, constructs and actionability

| Domain | Example constructs | Role in paper 1 | Actionable by a mentoring programme? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Identity and agency | self-efficacy, locus of control, expectations, aspirations | Mediator (not adjusted for in total-effect models) | Partly |
| Supportive adults | parent support, teacher connectedness, nonparental mentors, counsellor contact | **Exposure** | Yes (mentors), indirectly (teachers) |
| Family environment | household structure, economic hardship, stability, conflict | Confounder / moderator (context, not deficit) | No (context) |
| School engagement | achievement, attendance, belonging, persistence | Pre-exposure levels: confounders; post-exposure: mediators | Partly |
| Peers and community | friendship quality, participation, neighbourhood | Confounder / moderator | Partly |
| Psychological development | well-being, regulation, coping | Confounder (baseline) / secondary outcome | Partly |
| Transitions and opportunities | grade-9 transition, course selection, postsecondary planning | Timing anchor (H7) | Yes (navigation support) |
| Structural conditions | income, neighbourhood poverty, school resources, discrimination | Confounder / moderator | No (context), but shape programme design |

## Language commitments

- Family structure and economic hardship are treated as **contexts**, not deficits. A
  single-parent household is a description of living arrangements; any association with outcomes
  is largely mediated by economic resources, stability and other factors, and is not assumed to be
  causal.
- We describe *associations* unless a design supports more.
- Outputs are population-level. Nothing here is a tool for scoring or labelling an individual child.
